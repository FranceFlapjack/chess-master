## The rules

1. **Centre.** Put a pawn on e4 or d4 (as Black, e5 or d5). Pieces in the centre reach more squares.
2. **Develop.** Knights out before bishops, towards the centre. Do not move the same piece twice while others are still at home. Do not bring the queen out early; she gets chased and you lose time.
3. **Castle.** Usually kingside, usually by move eight. Then connect the rooks (nothing between them on the back rank).

Three moves you can play as White against almost anything: 1.e4, 2.Nf3, 3.Bc4, then castle. As Black against 1.e4: 1…e5, 2…Nc6, 3…Bc5 or 3…Nf6, then castle.

## Remember

- Centre, develop, castle. Ten moves.
- Knights before bishops, queen last.
- One move per piece in the opening. The opponent who moves the same piece three times has given you two free moves.

+++ Read more: why these rules work

Every piece reaches more squares from the centre, so the side that controls the centre has more room and more options. Development is time: each move that brings a piece out is a move the opponent has to match, and the side that finishes first can attack. Castling is safety: the king in the centre is on the open files that the opening will create. The Openings track takes these rules and turns them into actual openings; these three are all the theory you need to survive your first hundred games.
